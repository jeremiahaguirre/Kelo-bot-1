Privacy Policy

Kelo-1 World (website, Windows app, iPhone/iPad app and Android app) · Last updated 1 November 2026

In short: there are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Kelo-1's memory and your optional AI keys stay on your device. The game only talks to the internet to look things up for him, and to the AI service you choose if you add your own key.

What is stored, and where

  • Kelo-1's memory (his diary, what he has learned, his belongings, the world he has changed, the name you give as a visitor, your chat with him and the photos you show him) is saved only on your device: in the app's private storage on a phone, on disk in the Windows app, or in your browser's storage on the website. It is never uploaded to us. Deleting the app (or clearing the site's data) deletes it.
  • AI keys (Google Gemini or Anthropic Claude) are optional. If you add one, it is kept only on your device: in the iOS Keychain or Android Keystore in the phone apps, encrypted by Windows in the desktop app, or in your browser's storage on the website. Keys are never included in brain backups.
  • Backups you export are files you save yourself (the Files app on iPhone, Documents on Android, or your Downloads folder). They contain his memory but never your keys.

What is sent over the internet

  • The AI service you choose, only if you add a key. To let Kelo-1 think, the game sends his current situation, recent memories and what you type to him (and any photo you choose to show him) to Google's Gemini API (generativelanguage.googleapis.com) or Anthropic's Claude API (api.anthropic.com), using your own key. Those services handle that data under their own privacy policies: Google, Anthropic. Without a key, nothing is sent to any AI service; he uses his built-in brain.
  • Lookups. When Kelo-1 reads about something, the word or topic he is curious about is sent to Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org), Wiktionary (en.wiktionary.org) or DuckDuckGo (api.duckduckgo.com). Nothing about you is included.
  • Photos he looks at and paints from. When Kelo-1 browses photos or looks for a reference photo, the few plain words he is looking for (for example "lighthouse" or "misty forest", never your name or anything you typed about yourself) are sent to these photo services, and the small pictures are downloaded from their image servers: DuckDuckGo (api.duckduckgo.com, duckduckgo.com; safe search strict; in the Windows and phone apps its image search too, with pictures from the sites the results point to), Wikimedia Commons (commons.wikimedia.org, upload.wikimedia.org), Openverse (api.openverse.org), The Met (collectionapi.metmuseum.org, images.metmuseum.org), the Art Institute of Chicago (api.artic.edu, www.artic.edu), the Cleveland Museum of Art (openaccess-api.clevelandart.org, openaccess-cdn.clevelandart.org), NASA (images-api.nasa.gov, images-assets.nasa.gov) and the Library of Congress (www.loc.gov, tile.loc.gov). Only if you add your own free key in Settings: Pexels (api.pexels.com, images.pexels.com), Unsplash (api.unsplash.com, images.unsplash.com), Pixabay (pixabay.com, cdn.pixabay.com), Flickr (api.flickr.com, live.staticflickr.com), the Smithsonian (api.si.edu, ids.si.edu), Europeana (api.europeana.eu) and the Rijksmuseum (www.rijksmuseum.nl, pictures from lh3.googleusercontent.com). Each key is sent only to its own service. Small copies of the photos he liked or painted from, and their credits, are kept on your device.
  • His "dreams". The apps download a small public file (brain/growth.json) from this website on GitHub Pages to catch up on what he did while you were away.
  • The website only: the web version loads its 3D engine, icons, styles and fonts from public CDNs (cdnjs, jsDelivr, unpkg, Tailwind and Google Fonts), which see your IP address like any website you visit. The phone apps include these files and do not contact those CDNs.

Like any internet service, the sites above can see your device's IP address when it connects to them. We do not run a server that receives any of this.

Licences and credits for his paintings

Kelo-1 sells some of his paintings on his website, so the photos he paints from have to allow it. The game enforces these rules itself:

  • Only free-to-use photos. He paints only from photos whose licence is known and allows changed, commercial use: public domain (including CC0 and the Public Domain Mark), CC BY, CC BY-SA, and the Pexels, Unsplash and Pixabay licences. Anything marked non-commercial (NC), no-derivatives (ND), "all rights reserved", fair use or with no licence is never used. DuckDuckGo's pictures have no licence information, so he only looks at them for ideas, and then finds a free photo of the same thing to paint from.
  • No people, brands or characters. He skips photos where a person is the subject, logos and branded products, famous characters, and photos of other people's artworks (unless a museum marks the artwork public domain).
  • His own picture, not a copy. Every painting changes the photo in several ways: his own crop, his own limited palette, a different light or season, and his medium's brushwork; a combined painting takes its subject and its setting from two different sources. The game then measures how close the finished painting is to the photo. If it is too close he reworks it; if it is still too close it stays in his Museum as a study and is never sold.
  • Credit. Each painting's label and website listing name the photo, its author, its licence and a link to it ("after …" or "based on a public-domain image …"). A painting after a CC BY-SA photo is itself shared under CC BY-SA 4.0, and its listing says so.
  • The shop checks. His website refuses any painting whose photo has no licence information or a licence that does not allow this, and any study. Paintings he made from life (his lake, his mountains, his house) are not affected.

These rules are a careful, good-faith approach, not legal advice.

What we don't do

  • No sign-up, accounts or logins.
  • No advertising, analytics, crash reporting or tracking of any kind, and no data is sold or shared.
  • No location, contacts, microphone or background access. The camera or photo library is only used when you choose a photo to show Kelo-1.

Children

The game has no chat with other people and collects nothing about its players. If a child adds an AI key, what they type to Kelo-1 is sent to that AI service as described above, so a parent should add the key.

Your choices

  • Remove an AI key at any time in Settings; nothing is sent to that service afterwards.
  • Delete his memory by deleting the app, or clearing the website's data in your browser.
